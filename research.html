Proposed Doctoral Research

Embodied or Disembodied? Identifying the Persistence of Productivity Gains from Global Value Chain Relationships

A firm that becomes a supplier to a multinational buyer, or begins importing from a more advanced economy, typically becomes more productive — but it is rarely possible to tell whether that gain is embodied, lasting only as long as the relationship, or disembodied, retained as knowledge. This project uses firm-to-firm transaction data and a trade disintegration shock (such as Brexit) as a source of exogenous variation in relationship loss, to estimate the causal effect on productivity of losing a foreign trading relationship, and to ask whether a firm's position in its domestic production network predicts how much of that gain it retains.

Master's Dissertation

Heterogeneity and Non-Linearities in International R&D Spillovers

University of Nottingham — Supervised by Professor Facundo Albornoz Crespo

Tested a panel of 23 OECD countries from 1971 to 2019 to examine whether international R&D spillovers vary non-linearly with import intensity. Found that spillovers rise with trade exposure up to a point, then fall — consistent with a theoretical mechanism in which moderate import competition encourages innovation while excessive competition erodes the profits needed to sustain it. Self-taught the dynamic Common Correlated Effects estimator (Chudik & Pesaran, 2015) to address cross-sectional dependence, and constructed R&D capital stocks using the perpetual inventory method.
